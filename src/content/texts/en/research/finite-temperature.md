Most calculations describe molecules at 0 K, but experiments are carried out at finite temperature. At room temperature a cluster can be a **mixture of isomers** whose proportions change with temperature, and that changes what is observed in the laboratory.

This line builds **free-energy surfaces** from statistical thermodynamics and computes the **Boltzmann populations** of each isomer as a function of temperature. These populations are then used to average **infrared (IR)** and **vibrational circular dichroism (VCD)** spectra so they can be compared directly with experiments.

## Questions addressed

- At what temperature does the dominant structure of a cluster change?
- How does temperature affect the enantiomerization energy of a chiral cluster?
- Which isomers explain an experimental spectrum measured at a given temperature?

The approach has been applied to systems such as Au₁₀, Cu₁₃, Be₆B₁₁⁻ and Be₄B₈, and is complemented by *ab initio* molecular dynamics.
